# Opening lines leak; closing lines rarely do

No published college football model has shown a robust, after-vig, out-of-sample edge against the **closing** spread. The only peer-reviewed claim with a genuine temporal holdout, Coleman's 2025 five-system metamodel, beats the **opening** line, and only narrowly: 51.47% ATS on 2023–24 test games, rising to 55.33% on the 291 games where it disagreed with the opener by more than a field goal ([ResearchGate/Sage snippet](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394184848_A_predictive_metamodel_for_college_football)). The benchmark result is still Fair & Oster: rating systems, alone or optimally combined, contain no useful information beyond the final Las Vegas spread ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cwl/cwldpp/1381.html)). Ten seasons of independent tracking agree. No public computer system clears the 52.4% break-even over 2016–2025, and the closing line has the lowest absolute error almost every year ([Prediction Tracker](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=24)). Your own finding, that a Kalman filter on final scores adds nothing beyond the close and does not beat openers, matches this literature exactly; a score-only rating is the method the market prices first. The realistic target is therefore the gap between open and close. Four kinds of information have not yet been tested against openers: opponent-adjusted play-by-play efficiency, production-weighted roster priors for the early weeks of the season, QB-change deltas, and full score distributions for totals. Your repo already holds the data for all four. The ranked index at the end turns them into experiments graded against both lines.

## Ten seasons of tracking show the close beats every public system

The Prediction Tracker (PT) is the only systematic independent scorer of public CFB systems. Across 2016–2025, the best 10-season ATS records are **PI-Rate Bias 51.08%, Donchess Inference 50.52%, Massey Ratings 50.20%, Sagarin Points 50.14%, FEI Projections 50.12% and ESPN FPI 50.10%**, all below the 52.4% needed at -110 ([PT tables](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=25)). Absolute error tells the same story. Over 2021–2025, the **opening line's game-weighted MAE was 12.22 points**, against 12.48 for ESPN FPI and 12.49 for the system average. In 2025 the updated line reached **11.85** while the best computers sat between 12.08 and 12.39 ([PT 2025](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=25)). One-season spikes, such as FEI's 54.0% in 2019 and Massey's 53.2% in 2022, revert the next year. At roughly 800 games a season, one season's ATS rate carries a standard error near 1.8 points, so those spikes are what multiple testing alone would produce.

One detail matters for reading PT. Its ATS grades appear to be computed against a **midweek** line. PT does not document this, but the midweek row is the only line row without an ATS percentage, and the "updated" line itself goes 52.6–57.1% against the reference line in several seasons ([PT 2024](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=24)). A system that hovers near 50% against a midweek line is doing worse than 50% against the close. Even the systems' authors note the difference. Bill Connelly reported SP+ going 22-22-2 against the Caesars close but 20-24-2 against the midweek line in a single 2021 week ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/insider/story/_/id/32140951/how-sp+-computer-ratings-fared-vs-betting-spreads-cfb-week-1-games)). SP+ is not tracked by PT at all. Its record rests on self-reports such as "52 to 54 percent" historically ([Saturday Down South](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/?p=256192)) and 54% against early spreads four weeks into 2024 ([Connelly on X](https://x.com/ESPN_BillC/status/1837857641151778937)), and neither is independently verified.

The academic record follows the same split between open and close. Fair & Oster found that ranking systems carry independent information, and that an optimal combination beats each one, yet none of it survives once the final spread is included ([Sage](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1527002505276724)). Coleman's 2025 metamodel was trained on 2016–22 and tested on 2023–24, with 2020 omitted. It is "statistically significant in the presence of the opening line" and only described as "strong" against the midweek and closing lines ([LIDA record](https://lida.sport-iat.de/dfb/Record/4094978?lng=en)). Its 72.96% straight-up accuracy ([Sage](https://doi.org/10.1177/22150218251365223)) sits at the same ceiling as South & Egros's machine-learning models, which picked winners "over 70%" of the time without any ATS test ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.3233/JSA-190314?fields=title,authors,year,abstract,venue)). Three caveats apply to Coleman. The 51.47% headline test rate is below break-even. The 3-point disagreement filter is itself a researcher degree of freedom. And its numbers come from search snippets of a paywalled PDF. Its five member systems and their weights remain unknown.

### Play-by-play efficiency carries the information score-only ratings discard

The strongest public systems are opponent-adjusted efficiency models with a preseason prior. ESPN's FPI builds on EPA, adjusted for opponent and site. It caps blowout effects, and it prices rest and travel: **5.5 extra days of rest is worth one point, and every extra 1,000 miles traveled costs one point** ([ESPN Stats & Info](https://africa.espn.com/blog/statsinfo/post/_/id/122612/an-inside-look-at-college-fpi)). FEI rates scoring advantage per non-garbage possession, with explicit rules for excluding garbage possessions ([BCFToys notes](https://www.bcftoys.com/notes)). SP+ rests on success rate and explosiveness and treats turnovers separately as luck-prone ([Football Study Hall](https://www.footballstudyhall.com/2014/12/11/7377487/college-football-ratings-fplus-spplus)). The best-documented engineering evidence comes from the NFL. nfelo's weighted EPA uses soft win-probability weights, discounts fumble recoveries and defensive interceptions, and weights offense about **1.6:1** over defense. It reports roughly a **3.75% median lift** in out-of-sample R² over plain EPA ([nfelo WEPA](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/weighted-EPA-methodology-and-performance/)). For CFB, the public opponent adjustment is a ridge regression on offense and defense dummies plus a home column, with alpha around 150–200 for full seasons. Its 2019 home-field estimate was about 0.018 EPA per play, but nobody has published a test of its predictive lift ([RAD Sports Analytics](https://radsportsanalytics.com/blog/opponent-adjusted-stats-ridge-regression/)). These gains are not proven to beat the market. FPI's 10-year ATS record is 50.10%. What efficiency models do is lower MAE, which makes them the best candidate for supplying information the opener has not yet absorbed.

### Roster features matter most when in-season data is thinnest

Recruiting talent is the strongest single roster predictor of season strength. Bergman & Logan find the effect of recruit quality on wins remains significant even after school fixed effects absorb more than 25% of it ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v17y2016i6p578-600.html)). A 2022–23 practitioner regression gives recruiting r≈0.64 and transfer-portal ratings r≈0.39 with end-of-season Elo; portal ratings are not significant once recruiting is controlled for ([Beyond the Score](https://beyondthescoresports.substack.com/p/does-success-in-the-transfer-portal)). Returning production is the best-documented continuity metric: since 2014, teams returning at least 80% improved by about **6.4 SP+ points** the next season ([Yahoo/ESPN](https://news.yahoo.com/does-oklahoma-big-12-stack-203811037.html)). Its 2026 offensive weights are 39.6% OL snaps, 35.0% receiving yards, 22.3% QB passing yards and 3.1% rushing yards. Transfers are credited at their new school, with half credit for players moving up from lower divisions ([ESPN 2026](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/48259759/college-football-returning-production-2026-notre-dame-texas)). The national average fell from **76.7% in 2021 to 53.7% in 2025** ([ESPN 2026](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/48259759/college-football-returning-production-2026-notre-dame-texas)), so coefficients fitted before the portal era are stale.

None of these roster metrics has been tested against spreads. Because they are public and feed SP+ and FPI priors, their levels are almost certainly priced. Any remaining edge sits in two places. One is how the public metric handles transfers: it counts their production at face value, with no step-up discount from Group of Five to Power Four. The other is how fast the prior should decay; SP+'s prior weight "drops considerably after four games" ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/college-football/story/_/id/46128861/2025-college-football-sp+-rankings-all-136-fbs-teams)). The market-timing evidence points the same way. Bennett found that betting against the prior season's top-10 teams in their first game wins "significantly more than the 52.4%" needed, especially against non-Power Five opponents ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/atlecj/v47y2019i1d10.1007_s11293-019-09611-y.html)). Practitioners describe the first two weeks as the most volatile, with thin liquidity moving lines ([ESPN 2026](https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/49734086/college-football-betting-storylines-2026-season-ohio-state-texas-notre-dame-miami)).

### QB and availability shocks are priced fast but never studied

QB adjustment is almost entirely NFL practitioner work: 538's box-score VALUE regression, opponent-adjusted and fed into Elo ([538](https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nfl-predictions-work)), and nfelo's version measured relative to the median QB ([nfelo](https://www.nfeloapp.com/analysis/team-and-scheme-nfl-qbs/)). FPI's preseason prior puts a returning starting QB at about **3.3 points per game** ([ESPN Stats & Info](https://africa.espn.com/blog/statsinfo/post/_/id/122612/an-inside-look-at-college-fpi)). No peer-reviewed study tests whether markets over- or under-react to QB changes in either league, and claims of backup mispricing rest on cherry-picked streaks. CFB availability data is new and patchy: the Big Ten has required reports since 2023, the SEC since 2024, and the ACC and CFP since 2025 ([CBS Sports](https://picks-s6.cbssports.com/college-football/news/college-football-playoff-will-require-teams-to-provide-player-availability-reports-beginning-with-2025-season/); [The Osceola](https://theosceola.com/p/notes-acc-to-require-availability-reports-for-league-games-in-2025)). Bowls are a "race to information" according to Caesars' risk desk, with lines moving 2.5 to 9 points on opt-outs and coaching changes ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/espn/betting/story/_/id/47307193/college-football-betting-2025-bowls-lines-opt-outs-coaching-changes); [Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/college-football-betting-how-opt-outs-have-affected-bowl-game-betting-lines-200205644.html)). Spreads are also less accurate in bowls than in the regular season ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/jospec/v22y2021i3p251-273.html)).

### Score distributions are a pricing tool, not yet a proven edge

The best football exact-score model is Baker & McHale's NFL point process. Its scoring hazards can take the bookmaker spread and total as inputs, and it was tested out of sample with a Kelly strategy, but the P&L was not retrievable ([Salford](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1432547/forecasting-exact-scores-in-national-football-league-games)). Choosing which games to bet requires margin quantiles beyond the median, which argues for full distributions over point estimates ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10306238/)). Claims that team totals and halves are softer because books derive them mechanically are unsupported assertions ([PredictEm](https://www.predictem.com/?p=174841)). The CFB totals market itself looks largely efficient. Its only documented over-bias, in nationally televised games, had corrected by 2003–2015 ([Westga](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2008/football08.pdf); [J. Prediction Markets](https://ideas.repec.org/a/buc/jpredm/v3y2009i2p21-37.html)). A related NFL totals edge, unders at 47.5 or higher winning 59.7%, vanished after 2009 ([AABRI](https://jid.aabri.com/manuscripts/203263.pdf)). Wind is the one weather effect practitioners consistently find, above roughly 15–20 mph ([Deucescracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/nfl-weather-betting-wind-totals-handicapping)).

## The repo already isolates the one test that matters

Your Kalman result is a replication of Fair & Oster with your own data. It means final-score information is fully priced by the close and largely priced by the open. Coleman's open-only edge and PT's tracking point to the same structure. Whatever public-model information exists is absorbed between Sunday's opener and Saturday's close. So the most informative experiment is not "does the model beat the close ATS" but **"does model-minus-opener predict close-minus-opener?"** That is closing-line value, and it is measurable on every game with far less noise than ATS outcomes. A model that moves with the market has found information the market later agrees with. A model that also predicts the residual after the close (margin minus close) has found information the market never priced. That second outcome would contradict every source here, so treat it with suspicion until it replicates.

Three hygiene rules follow from the sources. First, every rating used as a feature must be past-only. CFBD season-level SP+, SRS and FPI endpoints are end-of-season unless a snapshot is confirmed, and joining them to games from the same season leaks the future ([PT on predictive vs retrodictive](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=24); [CFBD SRS](https://api.collegefootballdata.com/srs-ratings)). Second, CFBD's pregame win probability is derived from the spread, so it is the line in disguise ([CFBD](https://api.collegefootballdata.com/win-probability)). Third, the documented inefficiencies decay: totals bias, coach effects ([Westga 2016](https://www.westga.edu/~bquest/2016/football2016.pdf)) and the 2020–21 COVID-season anomalies ([Econ Bulletin](http://www.accessecon.com/Pubs/EB/2022/Volume42/EB-22-V42-I3-P139.pdf)) have all faded. Hold out 2024–2025 untouched. Count every variant you try. And run each pipeline on a placebo first (shuffled model-minus-line, or a random-walk "rating") to confirm it can return nothing.

The standard test protocol, used for every idea below, is walk-forward by week over 2014–2023 for development, with 2024–2025 as a single final holdout. Three tests apply, each run against the opener and against the close. The first is an encompassing regression, margin = a + b·line + c·(model − line), where the edge is c > 0 out of sample. The second is CLV: the slope of close − open on model − open, with a hit rate for direction. The third is ATS at -110 overall and in bins of disagreement with the line, with bins fixed in advance. Totals use the same three tests on points scored against the opening and closing over/under. Report Brier score or log loss for the cover probabilities so calibration is visible, not just hit rate.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the question rather than answering it. "Can a CFB model beat the line?" is settled for the close: no public method has done it reproducibly. For the opener it is plausibly yes, by a margin of a point or two of CLV that would pay only on high-disagreement games. Your Kalman filter is evidence that the score-only channel is exhausted. The open channels all involve information that is costly to compute well: play-level efficiency with opponent adjustment, transfer production discounted for level of competition, QB identity, and the shape of the joint score distribution. Each of these is publicly available in principle but unevenly priced early in the week and early in the season.

Two things are unknown and decide everything. The first is whether 2023–2025 openers have become efficient enough that even Coleman's edge has closed. His test set ends in 2024, and the 2025 opener MAE of 11.89 is the lowest PT has recorded ([PT 2025](https://www.thepredictiontracker.com/ncaaresults.php?type=1&year=25)). The second is whether CLV against CFBD's recorded openers is realizable, since the time and book behind an opener vary. Your strategy should therefore be measured in CLV first and ATS second, and a null result should be treated as a likely outcome, not a failure of execution.
